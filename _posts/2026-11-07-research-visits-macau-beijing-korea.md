A busy couple of weeks in Macau, Beijing, and Korea, with lab visits, a seminar, and IEEE SSRR 2026!

My first stop was [Macau University of Science and Technology (MUST)](https://www.must.edu.mo/), where [Prof. Hon Chitin](https://hengqin.must.edu.mo/id-16039/person/view/id-13481.html?locale=en_US) and his group warmly welcomed me. It was a pleasure to learn about their research, exchange ideas, and discuss possibilities for collaboration.

In Beijing, I visited the PKU-DS Lab at [Peking University](https://english.pku.edu.cn/) to meet with [Prof. Tong Yang](https://yangtonghome.github.io/) and his group. We had some very interesting discussions on robotics and embodied intelligence in industrial applications, exploring common research interests and opportunities for our groups to collaborate.

On October 30, my last day in Beijing, I had the pleasure of giving the seminar [“From Human Intent to Robotic Manipulation: Industrial and Assistive Applications”]({{ '/talks/2026-peking-1' | relative_url }}) at Peking University, invited by [Prof. Hangxin Liu](https://liuhx111.github.io/). Presenting our research in a building with so much history made the visit particularly memorable. Afterward, we headed to Delta Intelligence to see their impressive work firsthand. [Peking University’s seminar announcement](https://sai.pku.edu.cn/info/2330/11075.htm) is also available.

Then it was on to Korea for SSRR, where I presented [our work on language-guided grasping for mobile manipulation](https://eesc-labrom.github.io/agn_grasp/). I also had the chance to meet [Hyunyoung Han](https://www.linkedin.com/in/hyhan/) in person after getting to know each other through the IEEE Young Reviewer Program, where I served as a senior reviewer. It was great to take those conversations beyond the review process.

I also met with [Prof. Seokhwan Jeong](https://rim.sogang.ac.kr/advisor) from [Sogang University](https://www.sogang.ac.kr/), learned more about [RIM Lab’s work](https://rim.sogang.ac.kr/) and had a very productive discussion about future collaborations. Our conversation was another reminder of how small the robotics community can be: there always seems to be a shared colleague or an unexpected connection that neither of us knew about.

These visits were a great opportunity to share my research and the work we do with [Prof. Marcelo Becker](https://www.linkedin.com/in/marcelo-becker-761bb524/en/) in the [Mobile Robotics Group](https://crob.eesc.usp.br/pesquisa/laboratorios/labrom/) at [University of São Paulo](https://www.usp.br/), São Carlos.

Thank you to everyone who made time to host me, share their work, and discuss ideas. I’m looking forward to continuing these conversations and turning some of them into joint research!
